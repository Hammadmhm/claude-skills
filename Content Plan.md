# Content Plan — Since 1960
**Factory:** Architectural Hardware (Door & Window Handles, Knobs)
**Founded:** 1960
**Audience:** Arabic-speaking (Instagram primary, Facebook B2B)
**Series Name:** Since 1960 — منذ ١٩٦٠

---

## Series Overview

This content plan builds a brand awareness and legacy storytelling strategy around a 60-year-old Egyptian family-owned architectural hardware factory. The content runs in two tracks:

- **Instagram:** Emotional, narrative-driven short-form video (Reels)
- **Facebook:** Process-focused, B2B-credibility content for architects, contractors, and procurement

Every video connects back to a single throughline: *a factory built by a man who couldn't read or write, still running, still making, still here.*

## Episode 1 — الأصل *(The Origin)* ✅ COMPLETE

**Concept:**
The grandson — who shares the same name as the founder — sits at a desk and says directly to camera in Egyptian Arabic: *"I don't know how to read or write."* Cut to him on a bench: *"Not me. Him."* Camera drifts to the founder's portrait on the wall. Factory b-roll follows. VO carries the story. Final shot: the grandson's hand writing the factory name on paper, zooming out to reveal it fully.

**Script (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grandson at desk, direct to camera | *"أنا مش عارف أقرأ ولا أكتب"* |
| 2 | Grandson on bench → camera moves to portrait | *"مش أنا... هو"* — music enters |
| 3 | Factory b-roll, workers, machines | VO: *"حمّاد ما تعلّمش يقرأ ولا يكتب، بس بنى حاجة أكبر منه — بنى حاجة بقت موجودة بعد ما هو راح"* |
| 4 | Close-up: grandson's hand writing on paper | VO: *"حمّاد كتب تاريخ — من غير ما يكتب حرف واحد"* |
| 5 | Zoom out — factory name revealed | VO: *"وإحنا لسّه بنكمّل الكلام"* — hold — music swell — fade |

**Platform:** Instagram primary
**Tone:** Legacy, emotional, cinematic
**Length:** 45–60 seconds
**Status:** ✅ Filmed and complete

## Episode 2 — كل باب *(Every Door)*

**Concept:**
A montage of doors across Egypt — homes, offices, shops, buildings — each one opening or closing. The product is everywhere, invisible, taken for granted. The final cut pulls back to the factory floor: a worker's hands finishing a handle. The VO makes the connection.

**Hook:** Doors people walk through every day without thinking about what's in their hand.

**Script (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Street b-roll: various doors opening and closing — residential, commercial, public | No audio — ambient sound only |
| 5 | Worker's hands in the factory completing a handle | VO: *"كل باب بتفتحه، في حاجة وراه"* *(Every door you open, something's behind it)* |
| 6 | Handle placed down, factory in background | VO: *"إحنا اللي وراه — منذ ١٩٦٠"* *(We're what's behind it — since 1960)* |

**Shoot List:**
- Street b-roll of doors in real environments (variety: old building, modern apartment, shop, office)
- Factory close-up: hands finishing a handle, quality inspection
- Final wide: factory floor

**Platform:** Instagram
**Tone:** Poetic, brand-building
**Length:** 30–45 seconds
**Music:** Slow build, minimal, oud or ambient

## Episode 3 — الأيدي دي *(These Hands)*

**Concept:**
The entire video is extreme close-ups of one veteran worker's hands — never showing his face. Operating machines, inspecting a finished handle, passing it to a younger worker. Only in the final 3 seconds do we cut to his face. He's smiling. The VO is delivered over the close-ups.

**Why this works:** Humanizes the factory without sentimentality. Also communicates longevity — a worker who has been here for decades is quality you can't manufacture.

**Script (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Extreme close-ups of veteran worker's hands only — machine operation, inspection, handoff | VO: *"الأيدي دي شغّالة من قبل ما إنت اتولدت"* *(These hands have been working since before you were born)* |
| 6 | Hands passing finished handle to younger worker's hands | VO: *"والشغل مش بيوقف"* *(And the work doesn't stop)* |
| 7 | First time we see his face — he looks up, smiles | Silence — hold 2 seconds — fade |

**Shoot Notes:**
- Cast: the most senior worker on the floor
- Lighting: natural factory light, no artificial setup — grit is the aesthetic
- No face until the final cut — strict rule
- Capture hands in at least 6 different actions

**Platform:** Instagram
**Tone:** Human, craft, quiet pride
**Length:** 20–35 seconds
**Music:** Minimal percussion or none — let the factory sounds carry it

## Episode 4 — من إيه بتعمل إيه *(What It Takes)*

**Concept:**
A process video shot like a short film, not a product demo. Raw material enters frame. Follow it through every stage of production until it becomes a finished handle. No VO, no talking. Only factory sounds — machines, metal, rhythm of work. Final frame: the completed handle resting in a worker's open palm. Cut to black. Text appears on screen.

**End card text:**
*"صنع في مصر — منذ ١٩٦٠"*
*(Made in Egypt — Since 1960)*

**Shoot List (Production Stages):**
- Raw material / metal stock arriving or being loaded
- Cutting / shaping stage
- Machining / milling
- Surface finishing / polishing
- Quality inspection
- Packaging or final presentation
- Handle in worker's hand — final frame

**Platform:** Facebook primary (B2B) — also repurpose as Instagram carousel with stills
**Tone:** Process, capability, craft — no narration needed
**Length:** 45–60 seconds
**Audio:** Factory ambient only — no music
**Target viewer:** Architects, interior designers, contractors, procurement managers

## Episode 5 — اللي اتغيّر واللي ما اتغيرش *(What Changed and What Didn't)*

**Concept:**
Sequential or split-screen cuts contrasting the factory across decades. Old archival photo / same angle today. Old machine / new machine. A photo of an early worker / his grandson working the same station today. The VO makes the distinction between what evolved and what didn't.

**Script (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Archival photo: factory in early years | VO: *"الآلات اتغيّرت"* *(The machines changed)* |
| 2 | Same angle today: modern floor | — |
| 3 | Old machine photo / new machine | VO: *"التقنية اتغيّرت"* *(The technology changed)* |
| 4 | Old worker photo / grandson at same station | VO: *"الوجوه اتغيّرت"* *(The faces changed)* |
| 5 | Close-up of handle — old product photo / finished handle today | Silence |
| 6 | Worker's hands holding finished handle | VO: *"الصنعة ما اتغيرتش"* *(The craft didn't)* |
| 7 | Wide factory shot | Text on screen: *"منذ ١٩٦٠"* |

**Requirements:**
- Archival photos from the 1960s–1980s — *essential for this video*
- Same physical angles/locations shot today to match archival frames
- If no archival photos exist, this concept needs to be rethought

**Platform:** Instagram
**Tone:** Heritage, nostalgia, quiet pride
**Length:** 30–45 seconds
**Music:** Something timeless — classical Arabic or slow cinematic

## Episode 6 — Grandson Interview *(Unscripted)*

**Concept:**
Sit the grandson down in the factory — not at a desk, on the floor where things are made — and ask him one question on camera. Don't script his answer. Don't direct it. Let him speak.

**The question:**
*"إيه اللي جدك علّمكه من غير ما يتكلم؟"*
*(What did your grandfather teach you without ever saying it?)*

**Direction notes:**
- One camera, one take if possible
- No teleprompter, no prep — the authenticity is the content
- Edit to 60 seconds max, cutting only silence and repetition
- Do not add music under his answer — let it breathe
- Optional: a single b-roll cutaway to the factory mid-answer, then return to his face

**Platform:** Instagram
**Tone:** Authentic, personal, unguarded
**Length:** 45–60 seconds
**Why it works:** Episode 1 created curiosity about him. This satisfies it.

# Founder Story Series: *حمّاد* *(Hammad)*

**Status:** Source material gathered — scripts in development

**Series premise:**
A multi-part series telling the full story of how Hammad built the factory from nothing. Not a tribute video. A real story about a specific man, in a specific place, who operated by a completely different set of rules — and won.

The central tension of the series: **he couldn't read or write words, but he could look at any design once and replicate it perfectly.** He was illiterate in language. He was fluent in form. That is the thread that runs through every episode.

---

### What We Know — Source Material

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How did it start? | Started with his brother in a large workshop in Laban. Later separated from his brother, then built the factory independently |
| Why this business? | Personal ambition — he wanted to grow his production and build his name and reputation in the market |
| How did he run it without reading/writing? | No formal contracts — business ran on trust. "Take the good, give me the money." His word was the contract |
| Any near-death crisis? | No — the factory never faced an existential threat. It was built to last and it lasted |
| Who was he as a person? | Built himself from nothing. Known as deeply persevering. His primary drive was not money — it was legacy and name |
| Physical artifacts? | Early photos exist — to be filmed and incorporated |
| The most extraordinary detail | He could replicate any design just by looking at it — no technical education, no blueprints, pure visual memory and craft intuition |

---

### The Narrative Core

Most legacy stories are about overcoming hardship. This one is different — there was no single crisis. The story is about **a man who simply refused to be limited by what he couldn't do, and built an empire on what he could.**

He couldn't read a blueprint. He didn't need to — he memorized the form.
He couldn't sign a contract. He didn't need to — his reputation was the contract.
He couldn't write his name. He didn't need to — the factory wrote it for him.

That's the series.

#### S01 E02 — من غير توقيع *(Without a Signature)*
**Focus:** How he ran a business on trust alone — no contracts, no paper

**Story:** In an era where business was done on handshakes and character, Hammad thrived. No contracts, no paperwork — "take the good, give me the money." His word was binding because his reputation made it so. This episode is about a different kind of literacy — the literacy of trust.

**The hook:** Open with someone signing a contract. VO: *"حمّاد عمره ما وقّع على ورقة."* *(Hammad never signed a single piece of paper.)* Beat. *"ومحدش كان محتاج منه كده."* *(And nobody ever needed him to.)*

**Script Direction (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Close-up: hands signing a modern contract | VO: *"حمّاد عمره ما وقّع على ورقة"* |
| 2 | Cut to: factory floor, workers, movement | VO: *"ومحدش كان محتاج منه كده"* |
| 3 | B-roll: product being handed off, exchanged | VO: *"الكلام كان كفيل — خد البضاعة وهات الفلوس"* *(His word was enough — take the goods and bring the money)* |
| 4 | Worker's handshake close-up | VO: *"لما سمعتك كويس، مش محتاج توقيعك"* *(When your name means something, nobody needs your signature)* |

#### S01 E03 — من غير تعليم *(Without Education)*
**Focus:** The most extraordinary detail — he replicated designs just by looking at them

**Story:** This is the heart of the series. He had no technical training, no blueprints, no formal design education. But he could look at any handle, any knob, any architectural hardware piece — and reproduce it from memory, with his hands. This wasn't compensation for illiteracy. This was a completely different kind of intelligence.

**The reframe:** Every other episode says what he *couldn't* do. This episode says what nobody else *could* do.

**Hook:** Show a complex architectural handle. VO: *"شايف الإيد دي؟"* *(See this handle?)* Cut to Hammad's portrait. *"هو شافها مرة واحدة."* *(He saw it once.)* Cut to the factory producing it. *"وعرف يعملها."* *(And knew how to make it.)*

**Script Direction (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Extreme close-up of an intricate finished handle | VO: *"شايف الإيد دي؟"* |
| 2 | Portrait of Hammad | VO: *"هو شافها مرة واحدة"* |
| 3 | Factory producing — machines, process | VO: *"وعرف يعملها — من غير ما حد يشرحله، من غير ما يقرأ خطة، من غير تعليم"* *(And knew how to make it — without anyone explaining it, without reading a plan, without education)* |
| 4 | Finished handle in worker's hand | VO: *"في ناس بتقرأ التصميم — وفي ناس بيفهموه"* *(Some people read a design — some people understand it)* |
| 5 | Factory wide — workers at stations | Silence — hold |

**Platform:** Instagram — this is the highest-potential episode in the series
**Length:** 35–50 seconds
**Status:** 🚧 Ready to script fully — this should be Episode 3, after audiences are invested

#### S01 E04 — الاسم *(The Name)*
**Focus:** Legacy was his primary motivation — not money, not scale, but name

**Story:** He didn't build the factory to get rich. He built it to leave something with his name on it. In Egyptian culture, leaving a name — *ترك اسم* — is one of the highest forms of achievement. This episode sits with that intention and shows that it worked.

**The gut-punch:** End on the factory still running, still producing, still carrying his name — long after he's gone.

**Script Direction (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grandson at his desk (callback to Episode 1) | VO: *"كان همّه الأول مش الفلوس"* *(His first priority wasn't money)* |
| 2 | Portrait of Hammad | VO: *"كان همّه إنه يسيب اسم"* *(His priority was to leave a name)* |
| 3 | Factory name on the building exterior | VO: *"وده اللي عمله"* *(And that's what he did)* |
| 4 | Workers on the floor — the factory alive and moving | VO: *"الاسم لسّه موجود — والشغل لسّه ماشي"* *(The name is still here — and the work is still going)* |
| 5 | Grandson's face — he looks up | Silence — 3 seconds — fade |

**Platform:** Instagram — series finale episode
**Length:** 30–40 seconds
**Status:** 🚧 Ready to script fully

### Series Production Notes

- **Archival photos are essential** — film them properly (macro lens, slow pan, good light). They are primary assets, not supporting material
- **The grandson is the bridge** — he should appear briefly in at least E01 and E04 to bookend the series with the present
- **No reconstruction** — every image should be real: the actual factory, the actual family, the actual products
- **VO restraint** — each episode has at most 4–5 lines. The visuals carry the weight
- **Music continuity** — use the same musical theme across all 4 episodes so the series feels like one piece
- **Release cadence** — space episodes 1 week apart minimum. Let each one breathe before the next
