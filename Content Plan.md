# Content Plan — Since 1960
**Factory:** Architectural Hardware (Door & Window Handles, Knobs)
**Founded:** 1960
**Audience:** Arabic-speaking (Instagram primary, Facebook B2B)
**Series Name:** Since 1960 — منذ ١٩٦٠

---

## Series Overview

This content plan builds a brand awareness and legacy storytelling strategy around a 60-year-old Egyptian family-owned architectural hardware factory. The content runs in two tracks:

- **Instagram:** Emotional, narrative-driven short-form video (Reels)
- **Facebook:** Process-focused, B2B-credibility content for architects, contractors, and procurement

Every video connects back to a single throughline: *a factory built by a man who couldn't read or write, still running, still making, still here.*

---

## Episode 1 — الأصل *(The Origin)* ✅ COMPLETE

**Concept:**
The grandson — who shares the same name as the founder — sits at a desk and says directly to camera in Egyptian Arabic: *"I don't know how to read or write."* Cut to him on a bench: *"Not me. Him."* Camera drifts to the founder's portrait on the wall. Factory b-roll follows. VO carries the story. Final shot: the grandson's hand writing the factory name on paper, zooming out to reveal it fully.

**Script (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grandson at desk, direct to camera | *"أنا مش عارف أقرأ ولا أكتب"* |
| 2 | Grandson on bench → camera moves to portrait | *"مش أنا... هو"* — music enters |
| 3 | Factory b-roll, workers, machines | VO: *"حمّاد ما تعلّمش يقرأ ولا يكتب، بس بنى حاجة أكبر منه — بنى حاجة بقت موجودة بعد ما هو راح"* |
| 4 | Close-up: grandson's hand writing on paper | VO: *"حمّاد كتب تاريخ — من غير ما يكتب حرف واحد"* |
| 5 | Zoom out — factory name revealed | VO: *"وإحنا لسّه بنكمّل الكلام"* — hold — music swell — fade |

**Platform:** Instagram primary
**Tone:** Legacy, emotional, cinematic
**Length:** 45–60 seconds
**Status:** ✅ Filmed and complete

## Episode 2 — كل باب *(Every Door)*

**Concept:**
A montage of doors across Egypt — homes, offices, shops, buildings — each one opening or closing. The product is everywhere, invisible, taken for granted. The final cut pulls back to the factory floor: a worker's hands finishing a handle. The VO makes the connection.

**Hook:** Doors people walk through every day without thinking about what's in their hand.

**Script (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Street b-roll: various doors opening and closing — residential, commercial, public | No audio — ambient sound only |
| 5 | Worker's hands in the factory completing a handle | VO: *"كل باب بتفتحه، في حاجة وراه"* *(Every door you open, something's behind it)* |
| 6 | Handle placed down, factory in background | VO: *"إحنا اللي وراه — منذ ١٩٦٠"* *(We're what's behind it — since 1960)* |

**Shoot List:**
- Street b-roll of doors in real environments (variety: old building, modern apartment, shop, office)
- Factory close-up: hands finishing a handle, quality inspection
- Final wide: factory floor

**Platform:** Instagram
**Tone:** Poetic, brand-building
**Length:** 30–45 seconds
**Music:** Slow build, minimal, oud or ambient

## Episode 3 — الأيدي دي *(These Hands)*

**Concept:**
The entire video is extreme close-ups of one veteran worker's hands — never showing his face. Operating machines, inspecting a finished handle, passing it to a younger worker. Only in the final 3 seconds do we cut to his face. He's smiling. The VO is delivered over the close-ups.

**Why this works:** Humanizes the factory without sentimentality. Also communicates longevity — a worker who has been here for decades is quality you can't manufacture.

**Script (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Extreme close-ups of veteran worker's hands only — machine operation, inspection, handoff | VO: *"الأيدي دي شغّالة من قبل ما إنت اتولدت"* *(These hands have been working since before you were born)* |
| 6 | Hands passing finished handle to younger worker's hands | VO: *"والشغل مش بيوقف"* *(And the work doesn't stop)* |
| 7 | First time we see his face — he looks up, smiles | Silence — hold 2 seconds — fade |

**Shoot Notes:**
- Cast: the most senior worker on the floor
- Lighting: natural factory light, no artificial setup — grit is the aesthetic
- No face until the final cut — strict rule
- Capture hands in at least 6 different actions

**Platform:** Instagram
**Tone:** Human, craft, quiet pride
**Length:** 20–35 seconds
**Music:** Minimal percussion or none — let the factory sounds carry it

## Episode 4 — من إيه بتعمل إيه *(What It Takes)*

**Concept:**
A process video shot like a short film, not a product demo. Raw material enters frame. Follow it through every stage of production until it becomes a finished handle. No VO, no talking. Only factory sounds — machines, metal, rhythm of work. Final frame: the completed handle resting in a worker's open palm. Cut to black. Text appears on screen.

**End card text:**
*"صنع في مصر — منذ ١٩٦٠"*
*(Made in Egypt — Since 1960)*

**Shoot List (Production Stages):**
- Raw material / metal stock arriving or being loaded
- Cutting / shaping stage
- Machining / milling
- Surface finishing / polishing
- Quality inspection
- Packaging or final presentation
- Handle in worker's hand — final frame

**Platform:** Facebook primary (B2B) — also repurpose as Instagram carousel with stills
**Tone:** Process, capability, craft — no narration needed
**Length:** 45–60 seconds
**Audio:** Factory ambient only — no music
**Target viewer:** Architects, interior designers, contractors, procurement managers

## Episode 5 — اللي اتغيّر واللي ما اتغيرش *(What Changed and What Didn't)*

**Concept:**
Sequential or split-screen cuts contrasting the factory across decades. Old archival photo / same angle today. Old machine / new machine. A photo of an early worker / his grandson working the same station today. The VO makes the distinction between what evolved and what didn't.

**Script (Egyptian Arabic):**

| Shot | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Archival photo: factory in early years | VO: *"الآلات اتغيّرت"* *(The machines changed)* |
| 2 | Same angle today: modern floor | — |
| 3 | Old machine photo / new machine | VO: *"التقنية اتغيّرت"* *(The technology changed)* |
| 4 | Old worker photo / grandson at same station | VO: *"الوجوه اتغيّرت"* *(The faces changed)* |
| 5 | Close-up of handle — old product photo / finished handle today | Silence |
| 6 | Worker's hands holding finished handle | VO: *"الصنعة ما اتغيرتش"* *(The craft didn't)* |
| 7 | Wide factory shot | Text on screen: *"منذ ١٩٦٠"* |

**Requirements:**
- Archival photos from the 1960s–1980s — *essential for this video*
- Same physical angles/locations shot today to match archival frames
- If no archival photos exist, this concept needs to be rethought

**Platform:** Instagram
**Tone:** Heritage, nostalgia, quiet pride
**Length:** 30–45 seconds
**Music:** Something timeless — classical Arabic or slow cinematic

## Episode 6 — Grandson Interview *(Unscripted)*

**Concept:**
Sit the grandson down in the factory — not at a desk, on the floor where things are made — and ask him one question on camera. Don't script his answer. Don't direct it. Let him speak.

**The question:**
*"إيه اللي جدك علّمكه من غير ما يتكلم؟"*
*(What did your grandfather teach you without ever saying it?)*

**Direction notes:**
- One camera, one take if possible
- No teleprompter, no prep — the authenticity is the content
- Edit to 60 seconds max, cutting only silence and repetition
- Do not add music under his answer — let it breathe
- Optional: a single b-roll cutaway to the factory mid-answer, then return to his face

**Platform:** Instagram
**Tone:** Authentic, personal, unguarded
**Length:** 45–60 seconds
**Why it works:** Episode 1 created curiosity about him. This satisfies it.

# 🚧 WIP — Founder Story Series: *حمّاد* *(Hammad)*

**Status:** In development — awaiting source material and family interviews

**Series premise:**
A dedicated multi-part series that tells the full story of how Hammad built the factory from nothing — as an illiterate man, in a specific era of Egypt, against specific obstacles. Each episode covers one chapter of the story. Together they form a documentary-style archive of the factory's founding.

This is the most important content the account will ever produce. It should not be rushed.

---

### Information Needed Before Production

The following must be gathered through family interviews and research before any scripts are written:

- [ ] How did it start? — solo, with family, with a partner, from a workshop or direct to factory?
- [ ] What was the founding moment or decision? — why hardware, why then?
- [ ] How did Hammad operate without reading or writing? — who read contracts, how did he remember, who did he trust?
- [ ] What were the near-death moments? — the year it almost didn't survive, the crisis, the test
- [ ] Who was he as a person? — the real version, not the official one. How he treated workers, what he valued, how he made decisions
- [ ] What physical artifacts exist? — early products, tools, documents with his stamp or fingerprint, photos from the 1960s–70s
- [ ] What is the family's most told story about him? — the one that gets repeated at every gathering

### Production Notes *(for when ready)*
